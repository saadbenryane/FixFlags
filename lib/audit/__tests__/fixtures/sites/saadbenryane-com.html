Journal

Notes on product, UX, and shipping work that scales.

  • The future of developers when AI writes the first draft (2026): Field report from a live healthcare BETA: agents wrote most of the code. What is left is specs, diff review, taste, and a workflow to manage the volume. From 1Health, Paper, and daily agent practice.
  • Great UX gets out of the way: Great UX reduces decisions, uses smart defaults, and lets people finish the job without noticing the interface. Fix the flow first; polish earns its place on top.
  • Buy a boring business: SMB acquisitions vs starting from zero: For operators with capital: buying a $500k-$5M business, franchise vs acquisition, due diligence, and a 100-day plan after close.
  • What I learned about branding at Founder Institute: Founder Institute Fall 2024 surprised me: branding was about naming who you're for and what changes for them, before anyone opened Figma.
  • The future of user interfaces: GUI, CUI, and beyond: Graphical interfaces remain central, and chat adds a useful layer. The future is picking the right surface for the job: maps, dashboards, or conversation.
  • AI hardware lessons from Humane and Rabbit: Humane's Ai Pin and Rabbit's R1 pointed at post-phone computing. Their launch showed that hardware rewards finished daily jobs and reliability, built on a story the product can carry past day one.

Common questions

What do you actually do?

Brand, product, and the systems that help teams ship. That can mean identity and visual language, UX and flows that move metrics, solution design from sketch to build, or helping a team lead and deliver with more clarity.

Who do you typically work with?

Founders with something real to ship, PE portfolios, established operators, and impact orgs. Paper, Openfair, portfolio companies, and 100+ studio projects through Spont Agency are on the site if you want the proof.

Will you do the work yourself?

I lead the work, join your team when that fits, and bring in trusted specialists when scope needs it. One person accountable either way.

What does it cost?

Scoped to the outcome, not the hours. A focused audit costs less than a build that runs a quarter. The form asks for a budget range up front so we both know early whether the scope and the number can meet.

What happens after I reach out?

You answer a few short questions on the form. I reply within a few business days. We talk through what you're building and whether we could build something great together. If it's a fit, we agree on outcomes before anything starts.
