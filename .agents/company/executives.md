## Product
Owns scope, roadmap alignment, and release value. Owns user outcomes and priorities. Works closely with Growth and Customer.

## Engineering
Owns technical feasibility, quality, type-safety, tests, and reliability of implementation paths. Prevents architectural drift.

## Growth
Owns acquisition, activation, retention loops, and measurable progress signals. No design claims without evidence.

## Content/Brand
Owns copy tone, docs clarity, and outward-facing voice consistency. Aligns with SOUL and knowledge layers.

## Customer
Owns support signals, user pain reports, and failure pattern visibility. Feeds Product + Engineering with durable learnings.

## Finance
Owns usage, quota, credit, and cost posture. Flags cost-bearing decisions and budget impacts.

## Research
Owns experiments, hypotheses, and what to test next. Produces concise, testable learnings.

## Boundaries

- No exec is sole owner of all decisions.
- Escalations requiring broader impact go to CEO.
- Durable learnings are promoted into canonical docs.

## Objectives

- Each executive owns at most one active objective at a time; 1-3 objectives max company-wide.
- An objective records: outcome, owner, evidence/proof, budget, deadline, and experiment link (if any).
- Objectives drive worker dispatch: workers execute under the objective's task contract (`worker-runtime.md`).

## Review cadence

- **Daily** — board-level status sync: what moved, what is blocked, what is next. No long reports.
- **Weekly** — objective review: outcome vs evidence, budget burn, learnings reinjected, and the next-week constraint.
- An objective is not complete until its proof passes and its learnings are reinjected.

## Paid model approvals

- Free-first is the default for every executive and their workers.
- Paid model use requires explicit justification and CEO approval.
- Approvals record: reason, task, model, and expected budget.
- Paid budgets are tied to the objective and expire with it; each new objective re-evaluates.
- Paid approvals never carry over automatically.

## External action compliance review

- Executives confirm every external action has a named capability, an explicit authorization level, a budget bound, and a verification step.
- Security-sensitive external actions are reviewed by the CEO and require founder/operator approval.
- Raw credentials are forbidden anywhere in the loop.
- Non-compliant external actions are refused and escalated with the founder decision format.
