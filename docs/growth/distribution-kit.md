# Distribution and educational pilots

This is an execution kit, not a record of contacted partners or customer demand.
No outreach has been sent. Start with two relevant communities and one workshop
at a time. Use controlled examples or the public sample, never private customer
findings, for demonstrations. Keep the existing approved homepage headline.

## Supported introduction

FixFlags helps people inspect a published website, understand recorded Flags,
and prepare changes with evidence. Supported rechecks can show whether a recorded
condition changed. Explain partial coverage, protected actions, and unavailable
checks beside the result. Use the current pricing page for the beta offer rather
than copying a price into a pitch. Do not promise that every journey works or
that a suggested change improves conversion. MCP discovery remains withheld until
its independent acceptance requirements pass.

## Three ready pilot formats

### 1. Inspect a newly published page, 25 minutes

Audience: someone who has just shipped an AI-built website. Open a controlled
published page, inspect its preview metadata and unfinished text with the two
free tools, and explain what these tools leave unverified. Then show how a broader
website check records supported evidence. Reserve five minutes for questions.

Reading: `/guides/checking-after-publishing`, `/tools/meta-preview`, and
`/tools/placeholder-detector`. Success is an observed completed inspection or
consented analysis start, not attendance or an invented conversion rate.

### 2. Online does not establish that an action works, 30 minutes

Audience: a founder relying on a published app. Use a local controlled fixture
with a broken primary action. Show its recorded result, partial coverage, and
protected-action limits. Explain the difference between uptime, a scripted flow,
and the supported FixFlags evidence. Leave room for a manual check of the actual
business outcome. Do not touch a participant's production checkout or messages.

Reading: `/guides/website-online-but-not-working`,
`/guides/fixflags-vs-uptime-monitoring`, and
`/guides/fixflags-vs-scripted-browser-monitoring`. The native integration tests
exercise controlled repair and recurrence; they are not a production customer
case study.

### 3. Change, recheck, and interpret the result, 30 minutes

Audience: a builder maintaining a live website. Start from a recorded controlled
Flag, review its evidence and suggested change, make the change in the fixture,
and rerun the supported check. Include an unchanged state, an inconclusive state,
and a repaired state. Explain what recurrence means. A video or walkthrough must
show the actual observed sequence and label local fixtures as controlled examples.

Reading: `/guides/monitoring-ai-built-websites`,
`/guides/checking-lovable-websites`, and `/samples`. Do not substitute a static
sample animation for proof that a production repair completed.

and mature seven-day and 28-day Site cohorts with their actual denominators.
